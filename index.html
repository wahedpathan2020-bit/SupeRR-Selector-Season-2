Why Traditional Stats Lie

Standard strike rates reward low-leverage run accumulations (e.g. 40 off 20 in an easy chase of 120). Conversely, standard bowling economies hide garbage-time overs. Our Win Probability Added (WPA) framework credits performance strictly relative to the win-equity swing at stake on every individual delivery.

The Jaipur Home Fortress Factor

Sawai Mansingh Stadium features large square boundaries (75m+) and surfaces that decelerate in middle overs. RR requires spinners with flat trajectories who force mishits (Axar Patel, Krunal Pandya) and batters who hit true 85m+ maximums or possess elite placement (Buttler, Klaasen, Mitchell).

The Tri-Factor Composite Index

Rankings do not rely on reputation. Every player is evaluated on a rigorous composite: 50% Opponent-Adjusted Impact (via joint ridge regression), 25% Resilience (z-score vs own baseline), and 25% Consistency (Beta-shrunk share of positive matches).

📐 Rigorous Quantitative Methodology

How pressure is defined, calibrated, and measured across 200,999 deliveries.

Reproducible Pipeline

Stage 1: Out-of-Fold Win Probability

Trained separate gradient-boosted decision tree (GBDT) models for Innings 1 and Innings 2 across 766 completed IPL & T20I matches. Evaluated strictly via 5-fold match-grouped cross-validation so no match is scored by a model that saw it.

✓ Chase Model AUC: 0.89 • Calibrated across all deciles

Stage 2: Ball-by-Ball Leverage Index

Leverage quantifies the game-equity swing at stake. Computed as the expected absolute win-probability change across counterfactual outcomes weighted by empirical frequencies.

L_i = Σ P(k) · |WP(state | outcome k) - WP(state)|
✓ High Pressure: L ≥ 2.47 win-pts, WP 20-80%, Over ≥ 3

Stage 3: Opponent-Adjusted Joint Ridge

Solves the quality-of-opposition challenge. A simultaneous ridge regression models every batter (+) and bowler (-). Batters facing Bumrah or Rashid Khan are credited accordingly, while ridge penalty applies empirical-Bayes shrinkage.

✓ Prevents small-sample distortion • Shrinks noisy spikes

The Tri-Factor Composite Scoring Formula

Eligible players (≥80 high-pressure deliveries, ≥12 pressure matches, ≥8 qualifying innings/spells) are ranked by standardizing three orthogonal dimensions:

Composite Score = 0.50 · z(Impact) + 0.25 · z(Resilience) + 0.25 · z(Consistency)
1. Impact (50% Weight):

Opponent-adjusted Win Probability Added per 100 pressure deliveries. Measures pure winning contribution against tough opposition.

2. Resilience (25% Weight):

Player's own baseline test. Difference between leverage captured in high pressure vs low-pressure baseline play. Rewards those who rise when things get tight.

3. Consistency (25% Weight):

Beta-shrunk percentage of pressure innings or spells with non-negative WPA impact. Heavily penalizes single-game wonders.

Honest Check on Persistence & Model Limitations

Splitting matches into odd and even halves yields a rank correlation of ~0.17 for batters (n=94) and ~0.18 for bowlers (n=105), translating to ~0.30 under Spearman-Brown correction. This confirms a known truth in cricket analytics: pressure clutch is partially an innate skill and partially subject to high natural variance.

Innings 1 Coarseness: Without real-time pitch bounce data, the 1st innings model operates at 0.67 AUC vs 0.89 in chases.
Eligibility Cutoff: Players with <80 clutch deliveries were omitted. Rising stars like Vaibhav Sooryavanshi placed at rank 11 due to small sample size.
Cross-League Transfer: IPL scoring environments are denser than bilateral T20Is; ridge adjustment controls for opposition but pitch nuances remain.
